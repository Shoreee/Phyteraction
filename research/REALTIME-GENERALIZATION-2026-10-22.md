# Realtime non-destructive interaction and asset generalization

## Findings and scope

The local reference is the current working tree, including the previous hand/gun fixes. Source Gaussian/SH/material/boundary payloads are retained. Local measurements and temporary regression code/results live under ignored `artifacts/reports/realtime-cage-2026-10-22`; they are editor/M5 measurements, not Vision Pro measurements.

The dominant established regression was simulation scheduling and synchronization, not simply splat count. The earlier interactive implementation accumulated fixed time once per rendered frame, could run behind real time under a substep budget, and read reactions asynchronously. The recent implementation advances every 360 Hz hand tick at the released explicit MPM step limit (typically 1e-4 s), then waits synchronously for finite-body reaction. Previous measured active costs were 80–111 ms per 10 ms simulated for the two plants and 197 ms for sand. Rest-state elision did not help after contact. Restoring stale impulse replay or silently slowing simulated time would not provide correct simultaneous contact.

The nine released PhysFlow scenes have author material initialization, not optimized material results. They retain normalized geometry, source material units and released regions. The source configs include foam, metal/plasticine, non-Newtonian flow and sand: there is no generic fracture checkbox. A point/particle method without persistent connectivity can visibly flow apart even without an explicit fracture model. Sparse reconstructed shells, small hand features and partitioned articulated coupling add failure modes that material training alone cannot fix. PhysFlow demonstrates prescribed simulated actions; it does not supply a ready-made bidirectional articulated XR hand solver. See the [official implementation](https://github.com/zhuomanliu/PhysFlow/tree/6beba6746fad4590f5b63ce6fa85c3b862b182d9) and [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14423).

## Implemented realtime mode

The normal scene uses a connected voxel/tetrahedral material cage. Occupancy, diagonal bridges and enclosed-void filling come from reconstruction geometry; fixed regions come from author cuboids; the existing engine bearing is derived from the author rotation region and retains free azimuth. Static reconstruction/background is excluded from physical integration. All original splats remain in the renderer. Their centers/covariances follow persistent material coordinates; distant disconnected capture floaters use a bounded nearest-cell deformation plus their unchanged rest offset. Flower retains its learned modal implementation.

The cage consumes source E, nu, rho and mass, preserving the established normalized-to-metric E/rho time calibration. It does not infer metric material ground truth from the presentation size. The new mode replaces plastic/viscous/granular evolution with elasticity around the captured rest shape. Anchored geometry is load-balanced at rest; the sand replay's collapse load/release is not reapplied. This is a deliberate approximation to meet the requested temporary non-destructive behavior, not full PhysFlow rheology or a trained improvement. PAPER is unavailable by default; original MPM components/data remain disabled for research comparison.

The finite-strain energy is the stable Neo-Hookean form, with persistent rest matrices:

`Psi = mu/2 * (||F||^2 - 3) - mu*(J-1) + lambda/2 * (J-1)^2`, `J = det(F)`. Here the volumetric coefficient is `lambda = lambda_Lame + mu`, preserving the source small-strain E/nu tangent.

XPBD projects a single nonnegative energy constraint with compliance derived from `mu * rest_volume`. This avoids the separate hydro/deviatoric projection's nonzero-rest splitting drift. A determinant inequality guards orientation, without a fracture/plastic rest update. The relevant numerical basis is [Macklin and Müller, Stable Neo-Hookean constraints](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/publications/neohookean.pdf). A finite iteration budget remains approximate; a local orientation projection is not a proof against arbitrary global self-intersection.

An early prototype used point samples and depth/substep separation impulses. Tests exposed both point gaps and large launch velocities from initial overlap. The final contact path uses the oriented external triangles of the connected cage, with linear swept sphere/triangle-plane checks for fast fingertips. One full fixed step is advanced without explicit CFL substeps. Rigid mobility includes inverse mass, point inertia and accumulated force/torque prediction. Zero-restitution velocity projection stops inward motion without converting existing overlap into an artificial launch. Contact impulses/torque return to PhysX in the same tick. This remains a partitioned approximation to an articulated solve; joints are ultimately resolved by PhysX. It is not a replacement for testing fast, occluded and bimanual real tracking.

GPU ownership is unchanged: render resources initialize before simulation and release afterward. CPU cage contact needs no GPU readback. After eight contact-free low-energy ticks (approximately 0.1% cell strain and 0.01 cell/s), the solver sleeps at its current pose rather than snapping to rest; bearing orientation is retained. This is an explicit numerical settling tolerance, not a simulation-time budget. Only deformed cage positions are uploaded for Gaussian covariance transport once per display frame. The source point count and SH are unchanged. A small render-quality concession changes the faint-tail cutoff from 1/255 to 2/255; the source threshold remains configurable. This reduces transparent footprint/fragment work, not physical simulation time or advancing Gaussian count. It is not an adaptive resolution drop.

The [official license notice](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/WaterScenes/blob/6032de37ee5cccfe5d86b5cf0959f66747de9342/LICENCE.md) restricts tree assets to non-commercial use; the user explicitly chose research use. Payloads/derived assets remain ignored, with pinned URLs, hashes, license and restoration metadata versioned. The source mesh and atlas are imported rather than replacing the tree with procedural geometry. Original shader wind is disabled.

TREE uses the same cage/contact solver, with a mesh deformation adapter instead of Gaussian output. Geometry supplies occupancy/embedding and a root boundary; no authored bone rig or gesture-triggered sway is used. The initial scalar material profile is explicitly transferred from the published alocasia initialization, with provenance; it is not measured wood or learned generalization. This verifies asset and contact plumbing. It does not establish species/material fidelity or arbitrary-asset generalization.

## 2026 research and next stage

[i-PhysGaussian (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17117) studies implicit MPM with Newton/GMRES, larger stable steps and explicit globalization. This addresses the explicit CFL bottleneck but does not remove GPU/CPU articulated-coupling cost or provide physical material labels. Its reported results are not measurements of this implementation. A later full-rheology backend should compare its convergence/residual and hardware cost against the realtime cage rather than simply increasing explicit dt.

[NGFF / ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00148) learns a Gaussian force-field dynamics operator from synthetic trajectories and studies held-out transfer. Its training setup, boundary/collision modules and distribution matter: visual reconstruction alone does not identify arbitrary absolute stiffness or guarantee passive XR contact. It motivates a shared learned operator over material coordinates rather than optimizing nine unrelated scene scalars. It is not implemented here and no NGFF weights are claimed.

[Phys4DRT](https://github.com/OneCoin123/Phys4DRT) also motivates separating the visual representation from a lower-cost physical discretization. The present implementation uses an explicit documented numerical approximation, not that project's trained results or an unvalidated claim of equivalence.

A proposed next experiment is a material-conditioned force/residual operator over the persistent cage graph. Inputs should include metric geometry/thickness, material uncertainty, rest/deformed edges, velocities, boundary tags and relative contact state. Predict internal force or a warm start; keep a verified contact/volume projection and a passivity/work check in the loop. This is a proposal, not a published novelty or completed trained component.

Data should include calibrated force–displacement observations in addition to source video. Static appearance cannot uniquely identify material or absolute mass. Geometry-derived voxelization/root conditions are discretization and boundary metadata, not manual gesture animation; per-asset tuned stiffness is not a generalization result. Keep released initialization, fitted scalar material and learned spatial/operator outputs as separate provenance categories.

Split evaluation by **asset**, not frames of the same asset. Hold out TREE geometry/species and camera views entirely; include rotated/scaled shapes, unseen contacts and sustained/bimanual pushes. Report force/displacement/energy errors, penetration and recovery, orientation violations, finite-body momentum, contact latency, frame p95 and allocations. Compare initialization-only, per-scene fitting, a shared learned operator and the reference physical solve. Failing held-out TREE behavior must not be hidden with a tree-specific stiffness override.

Future rendering work should be driven by device GPU timing: projected-footprint compaction, tile/bin rasterization, SH/covariance precomputation and error-bounded LOD are candidates. A stereo cache must preserve XR late-latching and per-eye projection; simply caching CPU-camera projected splats risks head-motion latency. No such unverified rendering rewrite is included here.

## Final local validation

The final authored scene passed 47 play-mode checks: all ten explicit backend bindings, model switching/reset, prior GPU-buffer invalidation, TREE contact and repeated mesh ownership/release. Asset checks found zero missing scripts and zero broken serialized references. All 54 source payload/metadata hashes match the pre-change baseline. The eleven functional assemblies are acyclic; Tracking, Dynamics and Simulation retain their required business dependency boundaries.

On this Mac M5, a 25 g probe at 0.15 m/s over 120 fixed steps measured 0.019–0.947 ms per 1/360 s cage step across the nine PhysFlow models. These figures include this specific contact fixture and are not headset FPS, worst-case bimanual cost, or a claim of equivalence to full MPM. A free finite-body fixture had linear momentum residual below 4e-10 kg m/s. The plane bearing retained rotation (28.63 degrees in 0.5 s from 1 rad/s), with no welded bearing nodes and radial/axial error below 5e-9 m. A 15 m/s swept sphere test stopped at the boundary after two ticks. These synthetic checks do not prove arbitrary articulated-hand nonpenetration.

The same-view source/realtime tail-cutoff comparisons passed for all ten models: affected-image-region PSNR was 46.47–53.40 dB. Gaussian counts and source SH were unchanged. This is a local rest-view image comparison, not a bound over all deformed stereo views.

With Gaussian and licensed TREE payloads temporarily absent, play mode remained in LAB, disabled missing model entries and preserved RESET/BACK behavior. Both that configuration and the restored full configuration exported visionOS Xcode projects with zero build errors. Source payloads were restored and hashed again. Temporary validation assets/code were removed before the final full export; generated results remain ignored. Actual headset frame times, sustained fast/bimanual tracking, and contact comfort still require device validation. No training was started.
